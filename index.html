Notary Information & Services (Estonia)

The Role of a Notary in Estonia

In Estonia, a notary plays a crucial, impartial role in property transactions. Their key responsibilities include:

  • Verifying the identities of the buyer and seller.
  • Checking the legal status of the property in the Land Register.
  • Ensuring both parties understand the terms of the sale agreement.
  • Drafting and formalizing the official purchase agreement (notarial deed).
  • Overseeing the secure transfer of funds (often via a notary deposit account).
  • Submitting the application to the Land Register to transfer ownership.

Using a notary is legally required for property sales in Estonia to ensure validity and security.

After Sale Guidance

Moving Out Tips

  • Plan your move well in advance. Book movers or arrange transport.
  • Start packing non-essential items early. Label boxes clearly.
  • Notify relevant parties of your change of address (banks, subscriptions, government agencies).
  • Arrange for final meter readings for utilities (electricity, water, gas).
  • Ensure the property is clean and empty as agreed upon in the sales contract.
  • Coordinate key handover with the buyer or their representative.

Property Transfer Agreement

The formal transfer of ownership is handled by the notary and recorded in the Land Register. However, a separate handover protocol (üleandmise-vastuvõtmise akt) is highly recommended. This document confirms:

  • The date and time of physical handover of the property.
  • The condition of the property at the time of handover.
  • Meter readings (electricity, water, etc.).
  • The transfer of keys, documents, and any included appliances/fixtures.
  • Both buyer and seller should sign this document.
Download Handover Protocol Template (Word)

Final Checklist

  • Cancel or transfer utility contracts (electricity, water, internet, TV).
  • Update your official residence registration if required.
  • Forward your mail if necessary.
  • Securely transfer or delete any personal data related to the property (e.g., smart home devices).
  • Keep copies of all sales documents, notary deeds, and handover protocols for your records.
